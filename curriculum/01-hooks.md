# 01 · Hooks

Sources: reels ledger (batches 1-9), esp. @viralspymedia, @growthwithhardik, @yipeng_4, @ameliagonzamonte, @brock11johnson, @erika.prompts,
@adley, @marketing.mona, @marketingideas365, @ajaysinghdigital, @hormozi. Status: learned from reels, **not yet lab-tested**.

## Core principle
The hook's only job is to stop the scroll and earn the next second. Hormozi's priority order for any ad or reel:
1. get attention (hook, visual or verbal), 2. tell them what to do (CTA), 3. everything else.
If the first line is boring, nothing after it gets seen, so write the first line before you shoot.

## A hook has three layers, and they run at the same time
| Layer | What it is | Examples from the set |
|---|---|---|
| **Visual** | Movement or an odd image in frame 1, works with sound off | Walk into frame, sit down, lean into camera, take off sunglasses, close laptop, raise/lower arm fast (@brock11johnson). A pencil dropping toward a needle (@nothingdesign.or). |
| **Text on screen** | One line readable in under a second | "90% AI script's result" over tiny view counts; "21 saal, ek bag, aur [x] ka profit"; "S-tier ads (they should be ILLEGAL)". |
| **Spoken** | First sentence | "A $300 embroidery machine needed a subscription, so its owner built…" |

## Verbal hook types
| Type | Formula | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Basic | [what it's like] + [product] | "Apple juice with protein." |
| Outcome | How I get [result] while [easy thing] | "How I get 100 g of protein a day sipping this." |
| Call-out | If you [situation], you'll [consequence] | "If you sit in meetings all week and don't get enough protein, you'll lose muscle." |
| Numbers | Specific figures, no adjectives | "20 g protein, 90 calories, no added sugar." |
| Objection | The thing people say against you | "Everyone told me there's nothing in mushroom farming." |
| Loss / warning | N things that will hurt [named group], fix before [deadline] | "5 Instagram settings that will wreck you if you're a small account. Fix them before your next post." |
| Proof contrast | From this → to this | "Here's how I took a company's ads from this to this." |
| Curiosity gap | Promise with a missing piece | "…and the strangest part is who else gets to draw in it." |
| Ego / quiz | Let's see if you actually know X | "Let's see if you're secretly a marketing genius." |
| Controversy | This is going to be controversial | "Women and lust sell the most." |
| Escalation | N ways…, but it gets progressively more niche | "10/10 ways to become disgustingly good at running ads…" |
| Question the viewer is already asking | Their words, verbatim | "How do I go viral on LinkedIn?" |
| News / "someone just" | Someone just built X and made it free | "These guys just open-sourced a tool that…" |
| Story-compare | A is better than B, yet B wins. Why? | "Meera's candle is better than Kabir's, yet Kabir gets 40 orders a week and Meera 4." |

## Borrowed-attention hooks
- **Stitch hook**: open with an already-viral clip, then land on your point.
- **Anticipation hook**: a clip where something is about to happen (foot heading for a barrier, a step into a well of unknown depth), then cut
  to your video.
- **Ad break** (@adley): open the suspense clip, cut before the payoff, say "ad break, 10, 9, 8…", pitch, then return to the payoff. People sit
  through the pitch for the ending.

## Framing (same content, different first line) — @ameliagonzamonte
The same ring looks cheap in a plastic bag and precious in a velvet box: presentation sets the expectation.
| Device | Flat version | Framed version |
|---|---|---|
| Suspense | How to check a used phone | Watch me find out this "like new" phone had a swapped battery |
| Relatability | Tips for packing school lunch | For the parent who finds the lunchbox still full at 4pm |
| Stakes | Tips for product photos | If your first photo is dark, you've lost the sale before the price shows |
| Contrast | A before/after | A ₹300 print next to a ₹3,000 print |
| Authority | [the fact] | Ten years repairing these, and here's the one I'd never buy |

## Templates
Two creators' posts in the batch were long lists of fill-in-the-blank hooks (sales hooks grouped by psychological trigger, and
"negative" hooks grouped as unpopular opinion, blunt call-out, reality check and mistake). The lists themselves are not reproduced
here. The patterns they share with many other reels are captured, in our own wording and grouped by trigger, in
`skills/hooks/references/swipe-templates.md`. Triggers seen: curiosity and knowledge gap, frustration, loss aversion, desire,
social proof and specific proof, belief shift, objection handling, authority, call-out, honest negative.

What a strong hook does (common to several sources): it is specific about the content, gives a reason to be curious, names a clear
benefit, uses plain language and makes the next sentence necessary. Vague openers give no reason to stay.

## Where to find hooks (Hormozi's five sources)
1. Your own past winning ads. 2. Your own free content that performed. 3. Other people's winning ads. 4. Other people's winning free
content. 5. Platform ad libraries. Spread hooks across awareness levels unless you're targeting one.

## When hooks fail
- Hook promises something the video doesn't deliver → people leave and the reel dies anyway.
- Hook is about the creator or the news, not the viewer (see @griffinwooldridge1: "Anthropic just announced…", 37 likes).
- Empty intensifiers ("huge", "insane") with no specific.
- Negative hooks with no real insight behind them read as rage-bait.

## Rubric criteria to add (lab)
Specific? · Names the viewer or their situation? · Opens a loop that the video closes? · Works with sound off (visual + text)? · Could a
competitor use the same line unchanged? (if yes, fail)
