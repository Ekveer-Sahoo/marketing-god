# 04 · Positioning, offer, pricing and conversion

Sources: reels ledger, esp. @gigi.yaps, @montanez_nikoo, @primepulse.inc, @brandingtactics, @storyboard.magic, @dukaandidi, @makeithappen,
@andrei_breha, @partickfranco, @cierra.stacks, @theleylagreenhill, @winwithkritikaa. Status: learned from reels, **not lab-tested**; the
frameworks need their original sources read (listed at the end).

## 1. Be worth remarking on — Purple Cow (Seth Godin, 2003)
Drive past a hundred cows and you stop noticing. A purple one makes you stop, photograph it and tell people. "Being good is not enough; you
have to be remarkable", and the remarkable thing must be **the product itself**, not the advertising.
- Graza: olive oil in a squeeze bottle. Crumbl: menu rotates weekly. MSCHF: giant red boots.
- Book-jacket purse (@montanez_nikoo): rides two trends (BookTok + personalised fashion); every new book changes how it looks, so owners
  keep posting it. **Design the product so normal use creates new content.**
- Café flyer folded into a coffee cup on car mirrors, 10% off + QR inside (@primepulse.inc): Purple Cow applied to a flyer.

Test: would a customer photograph it or tell a friend without being asked? Is your brand "another cow in the field"?

## 2. Creative templates (Goldenberg, Mazursky & Solomon, 1999)
Claim in the reel: ~90% of award-winning ads follow six repeatable patterns.
- **Extreme consequences**: exaggerate the result of using the product.
- **Extreme situations**: put the product in an absurd context to show its power.
- **Pictorial analogy**: fuse the product with a symbol; a visual pun, no words needed.
- Competition · Interactive experiment · Dimensionality alteration (named in the paper, not explained in the reel; to study).

## 4. Offer
"You're not selling the product": build an offer people can't refuse, know exactly who it's for, and have delivery sorted (@cierra.stacks).
Study the ICP "like the bible": their day, what they spend. (Hormozi's offer work is the source to read.)

## 5. Pricing psychology
- **A win, not a discount** (@dukaandidi): the vegetable seller doesn't cut the price, he adds a fistful of coriander. "The price is fixed,
  but the matching earrings are on me." A cheap bonus protects margin and the price anchor, and the buyer still "wins".
- **Free shipping**: fold delivery into the price; a delivery fee feels worse than a higher price.
- **Odd / charm pricing**: 29 feels cheaper than 30.
- **Bundles**: one at full price, the second "free"; raises order value.
- **Endowment effect**: let them customise; people value what feels theirs.
- **Price anchor**: "agencies charge $20k for this"; "regular fee ₹1499".
- **Joke premium tier**: "hand-delivered by the CEO" as a story device (@eddiegotflow).

## 6. Conversion points
**Checkout** (@andrei_breha): anxiety peaks at checkout; reduce it with trust. Show customer count, three USPs beside the form, delivery
time, guarantee length, and one testimonial that answers the most common objection.

**Email-capture popups** (@partickfranco):
| Great | Good | Bad |
|---|---|---|
| Mystery discount · bundle discount · free gift | 10% off first order · free shipping | "Enter email for updates" · spin-the-wheel |

**Lead follow-up and cart recovery**: see `03-ads.md` §8-9.

## 7. Channels beyond Instagram
- **Reddit** (@zenushkascut): one aged account, no VPN; five relevant subreddits; 14 days of useful comments before posting (learn →
  comment → post); never hard-sell or drop links; send interest to a channel you control. Best for first paid users, easiest to get banned.
- **AI search** (@lawrenceaiseo): allow AI crawlers; content in raw HTML; Bing Webmaster Tools; answer in the first two lines; one facts
  page; schema that matches the page; one page per service named the way customers ask.
- **SEO keyword gap** (@udayan.builds): find keywords competitors rank for and you don't.
- **Outreach** (@adithya.reddy.k): people engaging with competitors' ads are warm prospects; DM with value; needs a strong profile.
- **Sourcing** (India): Surat (textiles), Jamnagar/Moradabad (brass, hardware), Morbi (tiles), Panipat (handloom), Kanpur (leather).

## When this fails
- Remarkable for its own sake (a stunt with no link to the product) gets attention and no sales.
- Bonus-instead-of-discount fails if the bonus looks worthless or the buyer only wanted the lower price.
- The "who buys what" table is a stereotype; replace with real customer research per product.
