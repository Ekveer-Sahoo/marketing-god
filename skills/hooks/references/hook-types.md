# Hook types

Pick at least six different types per batch. Examples are from reels studied in `curriculum/reels-ledger.md`.

## A hook has three layers, and they run at the same time
| Layer | What it is | Examples from the set |
|---|---|---|
| **Visual** | Movement or an odd image in frame 1, works with sound off | Walk into frame, sit down, lean into camera, take off sunglasses, close laptop, raise/lower arm fast (@brock11johnson). A pencil dropping toward a needle (@nothingdesign.or). |
| **Text on screen** | One line readable in under a second | "90% AI script's result" over tiny view counts; "21 saal, ek bag, aur [x] ka profit"; "S-tier ads (they should be ILLEGAL)". |
| **Spoken** | First sentence | "A $300 embroidery machine needed a subscription, so its owner built…" |

## Verbal hook types
| Type | Formula | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Basic | [what it's like] + [product] | "Apple juice with protein." |
| Outcome | How I get [result] while [easy thing] | "How I get 100 g of protein a day sipping this." |
| Call-out | If you [situation], you'll [consequence] | "If you sit in meetings all week and don't get enough protein, you'll lose muscle." |
| Numbers | Specific figures, no adjectives | "20 g protein, 90 calories, no added sugar." |
| Objection | The thing people say against you | "Everyone told me there's nothing in mushroom farming." |
| Loss / warning | N things that will hurt [named group], fix before [deadline] | "5 Instagram settings that will wreck you if you're a small account. Fix them before your next post." |
| Proof contrast | From this → to this | "Here's how I took a company's ads from this to this." |
| Curiosity gap | Promise with a missing piece | "…and the strangest part is who else gets to draw in it." |
| Ego / quiz | Let's see if you actually know X | "Let's see if you're secretly a marketing genius." |
| Controversy | This is going to be controversial | "Women and lust sell the most." |
| Escalation | N ways…, but it gets progressively more niche | "10/10 ways to become disgustingly good at running ads…" |
| Question the viewer is already asking | Their words, verbatim | "How do I go viral on LinkedIn?" |
| News / "someone just" | Someone just built X and made it free | "These guys just open-sourced a tool that…" |
| Story-compare | A is better than B, yet B wins. Why? | "Meera's candle is better than Kabir's, yet Kabir gets 40 orders a week and Meera 4." |

## Borrowed-attention hooks
- **Stitch hook**: open with an already-viral clip, then land on your point.
- **Anticipation hook**: a clip where something is about to happen (foot heading for a barrier, a step into a well of unknown depth), then cut
  to your video.
- **Ad break** (@adley): open the suspense clip, cut before the payoff, say "ad break, 10, 9, 8…", pitch, then return to the payoff. People sit
  through the pitch for the ending.

## Framing (same content, different first line) — @ameliagonzamonte
The same ring looks cheap in a plastic bag and precious in a velvet box: presentation sets the expectation.
| Device | Flat version | Framed version |
|---|---|---|
| Suspense | How to check a used phone | Watch me find out this "like new" phone had a swapped battery |
| Relatability | Tips for packing school lunch | For the parent who finds the lunchbox still full at 4pm |
| Stakes | Tips for product photos | If your first photo is dark, you've lost the sale before the price shows |
| Contrast | A before/after | A ₹300 print next to a ₹3,000 print |
| Authority | [the fact] | Ten years repairing these, and here's the one I'd never buy |

## Where to find hooks (Hormozi's five sources)
1. Your own past winning ads. 2. Your own free content that performed. 3. Other people's winning ads. 4. Other people's winning free
content. 5. Platform ad libraries. Spread hooks across awareness levels unless you're targeting one.

## When hooks fail
- Hook promises something the video doesn't deliver → people leave and the reel dies anyway.
- Hook is about the creator or the news, not the viewer (see @griffinwooldridge1: "Anthropic just announced…", 37 likes).
- Empty intensifiers ("huge", "insane") with no specific.
- Negative hooks with no real insight behind them read as rage-bait.


## Show-it hooks: products, and services or events with footage (use these first when there is something to show)
| Type | What frame one shows | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Transformation / proof contrast | The before state turning into the after state | Plastic print drops into the plating tank; same piece lifts out copper. "Plastic in. Real copper finish out." |
| Material reveal | The finished piece, then what it's really made of | "Guess what's under the metal." |
| Process | The machine or hands mid-action, one surprising claim as text | "Printing gold straight onto tulle." |
| Self-proving demo | The ad does to the viewer what it claims | "You just watched a pencil for 8 seconds. Now imagine that was your product." |
| Price objection | The price, said as the buyer's doubt | "₹499 for a keychain? Fair. Watch what it goes through first." |
| Gifting / situation call-out | The product landing in the situation | "Partner says 'kuch nahi chahiye'?" |
| Deadline | The date and the lead time | "Birthday next week? Today is the last day to order." (only if the delivery time is true) |
| Remarkable use | The product being used in a way people would photograph | A book worn as a purse |
| Own-footage anticipation | Your own clip, started two seconds before the outcome | Drone on the floor, kids crouched round it. "They built this yesterday. Will it fly?" |
| Testimonial / social proof | A real customer's words or reaction over the result (with permission) | "He has not stopped talking about it" — parent, last batch |

**Counting types.** For the "at least six types" rule, the verbal types, the framing devices and the show-it types are one pool. Near
duplicates count once (objection = price objection; anticipation = curiosity gap built on a clip).

## Awareness levels (spread a batch across them unless one is targeted)
- **Doesn't know the problem** → lead with a situation or a surprising visual.
- **Knows the problem, not the fix** → name the problem in their words.
- **Knows fixes exist, not yours** → contrast or mechanism ("unlike X, this…").
- **Knows you, hasn't bought** → proof, objection, price or deadline.
(To be deepened when we study Schwartz's awareness levels properly.)

## More types and rules from round 2
Added from the round 2 study (`curriculum/06-round2-synthesis.md`). Untested in the lab.

| Type | Formula | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Information gap | What they already believe + "but" + what they don't know | "You were told to post daily. But the accounts growing fastest post twice a week." (only if true) |
| Unfinished action | Start something unresolved, pitch before it resolves | A fuse is lit; the pitch runs; the cut never shows the end |
| Pick one | Two options, ask which | "Which of these looks more expensive?" |
| Confession | Admit a failure, then the turn | "I got this wrong for a year." |
| Investigation | I looked inside [thing] and found [specific number] | Needs a finding you can actually back |
| Old belief vs new belief | You think X. It's Y. | "It isn't your ad. It's the page it lands on." |

**Proof swaps.** Replace the empty word with the proof behind it:
| Instead of | Say |
|---|---|
| fast | the hours or days it saves |
| trusted | who trusted it |
| durable | what it survived |
| premium, high quality | the one detail a cheap version doesn't have |

**Rules**
- **Test the first frame before the wording.** The same line can do very differently on a static card and on something moving.
- **Decide the delivery** for spoken hooks: where the pause goes, which word is stressed, the energy of the first second.
- **Plan the re-hook.** Don't answer the hook in the next breath; ask a second question or raise the stake, then pay off. Say what the
  re-hook is in "Sets up".
- **A hook also turns people away.** Naming who it's for lets the wrong viewers leave, which is what you want in a paid ad.
- **Check:** "why would someone need to watch the next five seconds?"
