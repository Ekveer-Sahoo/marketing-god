# Ad formats

Pick formats the user can actually produce, and make the first wave span at least three. Sources are creators' swipe lists
(`curriculum/03-ads.md`); what works varies by account, so treat this as a menu to test, not a ranking.

## Video body formats
| Format | What it is | Needs | Good for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Demonstration | The product or service doing its job on camera; before → after | Footage of it working | Anything with something to show; price objections |
| Testimonial | A customer's words or reaction (with permission), or creator-style UGC | A willing customer or creator | Trust, higher prices, "does it really work" |
| Educational | Teach one useful thing; the product is the natural next step | Someone who can explain | Buyers who dislike being sold to; complex products |
| Story | A person, a problem, failed attempts, the turn | A real story (founder or customer) | Emotional or identity purchases |
| Faceless | Screen recording, text over b-roll, voiceover, animation or AI video | No on-camera person | Teams that won't film; quick variations |

## Static format families
Why these work: familiar interface reads as content, not an ad; rough or odd visuals break the pattern of polished ads; comparisons and
call-outs make the point in one glance.

| Family | Examples | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Native UI | Notes-app note · phone reminder or notification · chat or text thread · search bar with the question typed · email · social post or story reply | Must be obviously the brand speaking, not a fabricated real conversation |
| Editorial | Headline-style statement · "study / stat" card · founder letter | No fake news outlets or invented studies |
| Review / proof | Testimonial grid · real review screenshot · receipt or order count · "what I ordered vs what I got" | Real reviews only; the "zero-star review that turns out positive" format needs to be clearly playful |
| Ugly on purpose | Plain white background with basic text · hand-drawn doodle · whiteboard · handwritten note · "found / missing" poster | Still must be readable in one second |
| Comparison | "5 reasons why" table · us vs the usual option · graph · "X, but for Y" · tier list | Fair comparisons; no naming competitors falsely |
| Call-out | Names the situation ("For parents of class 7-10 kids in Bhubaneswar") · "N signs…" · "Avoid this" · myth vs truth | Address the situation, never assert a personal attribute |
| Offer / object | Product with one bold claim · bundle shot · starter-pack layout · free-gift or deadline card · meme (only where the audience shares the joke) | True deadlines and limits only |

## AI-generated video styles (for the video module)
Fake-podcast clip · talking object · whiteboard explainer · claymation or stop-motion look · paper or crochet craft look · low-poly or
cartoon story · cinematic product reveal · fly-through reveal from a real storefront or product photo · assemble-from-pieces reveal.
Use the real product or place as the first frame where possible; it keeps generated video believable. Don't copy a named show's,
studio's or toy brand's look.

## Format by buyer and price (a creator's rule of thumb; test it)
| Buyer / price | Lean toward |
|---|---|
| Older buyers | Text-heavy and native-looking images |
| Millennials | Direct response, call-out and offer ads |
| Gen Z | Educational, UGC, memes |
| Low price, impulse | Statics |
| Higher price, considered | Video, testimonial, demonstration |

## What counts as a different ad
Different concept, different format, or a different opening three seconds. Not different: a new background colour, a reworded headline on
the same layout, the same clip with a different caption.

## Match the demonstration to the claim (round 2)
Added from the round 2 study (`curriculum/06-round2-synthesis.md`). Untested in the lab.
| Claim | Show |
|---|---|
| It transforms something | Before and after, ideally side by side in one frame |
| It lasts | Someone trying to break it |
| It's good value | It next to something costly that does the same job |
| It's easy | The whole use in one unbroken take |
| A benefit you can't see | A visible stand-in for it (damage, wear, time) |
| It takes real work to make | The work itself |
