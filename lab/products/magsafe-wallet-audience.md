## Summary
- **Best first segment:** 18-30, iPhone 12 or newer, in India, who already personalises their phone (cases, charms, skins) and wants
  something nobody else has; buys on Instagram by DM. Close second: someone buying a personalised gift (name, photo, couple or pet design).
- **Not for:** people who want the cheapest plain wallet (marketplaces win on price), Android users without a magnetic ring or case,
  buyers who need it tomorrow.
- **Top problem or objection:** "Will the print rub off, and will the wallet fall off my phone?" (objection)
- **Lead angle:** a print you can feel: raised texture, made from your own design, one piece at a time.

## The eight parts (all `[assumed]`; replace with real evidence)
### 1. Problems
- My phone and accessories look like everyone else's.
- Personalised gifts under ₹1,000 look cheap (printed mugs, flat stickers).
- I carry a separate wallet for two cards.

### 2. Frustrations with current options
- Phone skins and flat prints peel or fade.
- "Custom" usually means a name in a stock font.
- Cheap magnetic wallets slide off or drop cards.

### 3. Desired outcomes
- Someone asks "where did you get that?"
- A gift that is obviously made for one person.
- Cards and phone in one hand.

### 4. Repeated questions before buying (expected)
- Price? · How many cards? · Will it fall off? · Does it work with my case / my phone? · Will the print scratch off?
- Can you print my photo / logo / name? · How long does it take? · COD?

### 5. Common mistakes
- Buying a magnetic wallet for a phone or case with no magnet ring.
- Sending a low-resolution image and expecting a sharp print.

### 6. Objections and limiting beliefs
- It's just a printed sticker. · The magnet will be weak. · Too expensive for a card holder. · Custom means slow.

### 7. Emotional reasons underneath
- Wanting to be seen as having taste; pride of owning a one-off.
- For gifts: showing effort without spending a lot.

### Angles
1. A print you can feel → run a nail over it; believable because the raised texture is visible and audible on camera.
2. Your design, one piece → send a photo, name or logo; believable because the printer is shown making that exact piece.
3. Not a sticker → scratch, rub and shake tests on camera (only once the tests have been done and passed).
4. A gift made for one person → their name, pet or photo in texture; believable with a real unboxing reaction (with permission).
5. Team or fest merch that isn't a T-shirt → weakest; needs a bulk price and one finished batch first.
